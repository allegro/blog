Effective November 1, 2026, I’m quitting my role as the editor-in-chief of the [Allegro](https://allegro.tech) Tech Blog.
Nothing bad has happened. I still enjoy the job, and I’m quitting because I can, not because I have to.
I think both people and companies should be doing more of this.

## A Brief and Personal History of the Blog

I became involved with the Tech Blog shortly after joining Allegro in 2014, when the blog was just a month old. Allegro was undergoing a technological
revolution at the time, [migrating a PHP-based monolith to Java microservices]({% post_url 2024-04-12-ten-years-microservices %}), and we wanted to share our
experiences in this new world with other developers.

I started by writing blog posts, the first of which was [The Memory Game]({% post_url 2014-10-08-The-memory-game %}). After a while, I joined the editorial
team and became editor-in-chief in 2018. We chose to blog in English rather than Polish to reach a broader audience, and we formed an editorial team to
maintain the quality of the posts. We tried to help authors improve their work rather than act as gatekeepers. The team also sought new authors and encouraged
them to write. The blog has always been a grassroots initiative run by engineers for engineers. The roles of editor and editor-in-chief were always unofficial
and don’t appear in any company documents.

Over the years, we published more than 256 posts on various technologies, conferences, team-building, and the inner workings of Allegro.
We wrote about successes and failures, published postmortems, and described solutions that worked as well as those that didn’t.
I consider it a measure of our success that Allegro’s current CTPO, [David Roberts](https://www.linkedin.com/in/david-w-roberts/), mentioned having read the
Allegro Tech Blog while at Amazon, long before joining Allegro.

## Knowing When to Quit

I’ve been editor-in-chief for eight years now. Over time, even well-intentioned people become entrenched in their ways, and the project becomes stale. I don’t
want that to happen to the blog. Stepping down now, while things are still going well, will allow new people to take over smoothly.
[Kasia Zadurska]({% link authors/katarzyna.zadurska/index.md %}) will become the new editor-in-chief, and the rest of the editorial team will support her.
Freeing up some time will allow me to focus better on other projects. I will remain available to help with the blog, but only as much as the team wants me to.
I’ve seen people step down in name only, so I will do my best to avoid becoming a backseat driver.
